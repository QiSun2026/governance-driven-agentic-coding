# Case Note: Closing an Engineered Product Without Losing the Learning

## The situation

An experimental AI-Native Systems Harness was built to explore whether
outcome-first contracts, governed Agent roles, evidence rules and gate records, controlled
learning, context budgets, platform adapters, and deterministic reports should
exist as one standalone open-source product.

The engineering work produced a frozen runnable prototype and bounded internal
verification. It did not produce evidence of external adoption, unaided use,
production effectiveness, or willingness to accept the added workflow
friction.

The Owner archived the standalone product track instead of treating completed
engineering as proof of product value.

## What the closeout did

1. Separated engineering facts from the product judgment.
2. Preserved the reproducible prototype and evidence history without keeping an
   active roadmap.
3. Compared proposed method changes with public GDAC v1.4 rather than assuming
   that every implemented control was new.
4. Tested whether a narrower candidate prevented a material state error beyond
   the existing method baseline.
5. Recorded a negative first prospective result instead of promoting the
   candidate because work had already been invested.

## The transfer result

The review did **not** establish a distinct new method rule or justify importing
the Harness runtime into GDAC. The first prospective comparison also found no
additive prevention beyond the existing GDAC baseline.

The reusable output was therefore smaller than the product:

- an optional Outcome Contract for material Agent work;
- an evidence-gated Project Closeout record; and
- a Prospective Method-Change Re-test that compares a candidate with the
  current method before promotion.

These practice tools were not evidence that the Harness product succeeded and,
at closeout, did not by themselves justify a new GDAC version. Subsequent v2.0
work incorporated only the parts that survived a separate continuity review,
adversarial review, and executable record-chain test.

## Why this is useful

Governance should help a team stop as well as start. Without a controlled
closeout, an Agent-built project can survive because:

- many tests passed;
- the interface looks finished;
- the team cannot bear to discard sunk cost;
- no one recorded an explicit negative decision; or
- useful implementation details are confused with a viable product.

The closeout pattern preserves learning while removing the false choice between
“continue everything” and “delete everything.”

## Claims this case does not support

- that the Harness had zero potential users;
- that its controls were ineffective;
- that its internal test results prove real-world value;
- that the proposed method candidates are canonical GDAC rules;
- that GDAC is externally or industry validated; or
- that any system is production-ready, compliant, certified, or independently
  assured.

## Decision rule to reuse

> Stop the product when the product evidence fails its continuation gate.
> Preserve only the artifacts and learning that survive a separate evidence
> test. Promote no cross-project rule without recorded baseline comparison,
> preventive value, and the required authority.
