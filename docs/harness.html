Definition

What the Harness controls

The Harness sits around Codex, Claude, or another software Agent. It does not replace the Agent runtime. It defines how work enters, what may change, what must be recorded, how claims are checked, when work stops, and what reaches the Owner.

The durable layer

Tools and models may change.

Outcome, authority, and evidence persist

Roles are temporary responsibilities, not permanent departments. The contract, permissions, records, evidence, and accountable decision survive each Agent session.

Two paths stay separate

The execution path may change the governed artifact within scope. The evaluation path may inspect, test, challenge, and block, but it may not rewrite the work or accept it for the Owner.

What exists here

Repository scope today.

Included

The architecture, Eval Rules, schemas, templates, validators, and worked examples.

Not included

An Agent runner, persistent evidence database, operating-system sandbox, deployment system, Owner identity service, or hosted control plane.

The design aims to make delegated work bounded, reviewable, portable, and economical. Those aims are not evidence of adoption or effectiveness.

Core functions

Five functions make it useful

These are logical functions. A small project can implement them with files and scripts; they do not need separate services.

Contract validation
Checks the outcome, non-goals, permissions, budget, stop rules, and Eval Plan before material work begins.
Attempt and budget record
Preserves material actions, snapshots, failures, unresolved items, and resource use across the whole task.
Evidence interface
Stores grader output in a common record linked to one claim or risk and one exact Candidate Binding.
Gate engine
Reads stored results and applies the declared rules. It does not alter evidence or make the Owner's decision.
Owner decision record
Binds the accountable decision, conditions, and remaining risk to the exact contract, candidate, and gate result.

Adapters, dashboards, model calls, databases, Agent hosting, and deployment are optional extensions, not part of the minimum core.

Architecture

One task, connected controls

The system follows one governed object from the Owner's intent to a recorded decision. Every handoff uses shared, versioned records.

Accountable control Owner

Sets the outcome, risk limits, authority, budget, stop rules, and final acceptance.

Outcome Contract What must be true, what is out of scope, and what the Agent may change.
Eval Plan Which claims are checked, how the subject is selected, and which evidence is required.
Write path

Execution

The Orchestrator prepares bounded work. The Builder makes the smallest change inside declared scope.

Shared source of truth

Records and evidence

The exact snapshot, diff, attempts, tests, logs, findings, limitations, and unknowns remain available to every role.

Read path

Verification and challenge

The Verifier checks frozen criteria. Red Team looks for unsafe assumptions, abnormal cases, overreach, and evidence gaps.

Harness Gate Returns one aggregate state: blocked or ready.
Decision package Shows the change, evidence, findings, unknowns, conditions, and remaining risk.
Owner disposition Accept, accept with conditions, rework, pause, or stop.

Read-only views may summarize the same records for the Owner, learning, or public communication. A view may reduce reading effort, but it may not rewrite original evidence or present a plan as a completed fact.

Authority

Responsibility is separated on purpose

Small tasks may combine roles, but write access, review context, evidence, and Owner-reserved decisions must remain explicit.

Authority boundaries for each Harness responsibility
Responsibility May do May not do
Owner Freeze the outcome, risk, authority, and acceptance. Review assumptions, unknowns, and remaining risk. Make final, release, and publication decisions. Treat required missing evidence as a technical pass or transfer accountability to an Agent.
Orchestrator Create bounded task packets, allocate context and budget, and enforce dependencies and stop rules. Expand scope, permission, budget, acceptance, or its own authority.
Builder Modify declared objects, test the change, and record implementation evidence. Write outside scope, weaken the Eval Plan, perform the only final review, or declare acceptance.
Verifier Run the frozen acceptance, capability, and regression checks against the exact snapshot. Modify the implementation, replace required evidence, or redefine success.
Red Team Challenge assumptions, edge cases, abuse paths, and evidence sufficiency without inheriting the Builder's reasoning. Claim third-party independence when it does not exist or make the Owner decision.
Integrator Reconcile the frozen artifact, evidence, findings, and handoff after required gates. Merge blocked work, suppress findings, or turn a failed gate into a pass.

No role creates its own authority. Every material state change must trace to a declared permission and sufficient evidence.

Rules that do not bend

Freeze before judging

Freeze the Outcome Contract, Eval Plan, and subject-selection rule before implementation output is evaluated. A material change creates a new version and reruns affected checks.

Deny undeclared action

Writes, external actions, role expansion, permission growth, and extra budget are denied unless the Owner has declared them.

Do not average away gaps

Trace each material claim through its eval, grader, evidence, gate, and Owner disposition. A score cannot hide failed, missing, or unevaluated required evidence.

Count the whole task

Usage by the Builder, child Agents, Verifier, Red Team, and reruns all counts against the task budget. Builder self-checks do not become separate review by changing the label.

Preserve the history

Keep prior contracts, candidates, attempts, findings, results, gate states, and decisions when a revision creates a new version.

Keep acceptance human

The Harness Gate prepares a decision. It does not accept, deploy, publish, or assume the Owner's accountability.

Record chain

Six record groups carry the task

The chain binds intent, work, evidence, and decision to the same versioned object. Summaries are views; the original records remain the source of truth.

  1. Outcome Contract Owner intent, outcome, non-goals, authority, task limits, budget, stop rules, and acceptance.
  2. Eval Plan Claims, subject-selection rules, graders, pass rules, evidence, producer limits, retries, and retention.
  3. Attempt Record Task packet, actions, failures, repairs, usage, unresolved items, and order of events.
  4. Candidate Binding The exact artifact, digest, source revision, and attempt under review. A changed subject requires a new binding.
  5. Gate Record Attempts, evidence references and digests, eval results, contradictions, findings, and the derived Harness Gate.
  6. Owner Disposition and Closeout The decision, conditions, remaining risk, retained work, prohibited claims, and any learning candidate.
Record integrity

A shorter summary may reorganize the material for a reader. It may not delete the original contract, candidate, tests, logs, findings, disagreement, authority, or decision history.

States

Checking is not accepting

Technical evaluation, gate aggregation, and accountable decision-making use separate state sets so one cannot quietly stand in for another.

Technical eval
pass, fail, insufficient_evidence, or not_evaluated
Harness Gate
blocked or ready
Owner disposition
accept, accept_with_conditions, rework, pause, or stop
  • aExecution requires a frozen Outcome Contract and the applicable Eval Plan.
  • bA required failure, evidence gap, or unevaluated claim makes the current candidate blocked.
  • cOnly ready work may lead to acceptance. Conditions cannot carry a failed or missing mandatory eval.
  • dA blocked candidate may be reworked, paused, or stopped. Rework preserves the failed attempt and creates a new Candidate Binding.
  • eA changed contract, plan, candidate, pass rule, or relevant procedure creates a new version and reruns affected required evals.

Learning is controlled too

candidateA failure or correction proposes a possible rule.
→
validatedA preregistered comparison met its rule; source evidence, counterexamples, burden, scope, and expiry are retained.
→
activeThe Owner authorizes the rule for an explicit scope.
→
retiredLater evidence or changed conditions remove it from use.

Validation does not activate a rule. Active status additionally requires an Owner authorization reference and an explicit scope; an Agent cannot supply its own authority.

New projects retrieve only the three to five active learnings most relevant to their task and risk, not the full learning history.

Economy

Spend control where it changes risk

Context, tokens, retries, Agent depth, and evaluation effort are governed resources. More roles and more tests are not automatically better.

Context

Load only what the responsibility needs.

Retrieve progressively

Start from the frozen contract and the smallest relevant source set. Add context only when the task or evidence requires it.

Checkpoint before context drifts

Preserve the current state, evidence, and unresolved items before the working context becomes ambiguous or expensive.

Whole-task budget

Retries are not free resets.

Set limits before execution

Declare time, token, build-retry, evaluator-rerun, trial, and Agent-depth limits for the entire task.

Stop repeated failure

Preserve material retries as separate attempts. Stop when the same blocker repeats or a budget is exhausted.

Risk and evidence

The smallest sufficient control set.

Load controls by material risk

The Outcome Contract selects a risk tier. The Eval Rules define the default profile; weaker evidence needs recorded Owner rationale and cannot be described as equivalent.

Measure only what is recorded

Record tokens per accepted outcome only when usage and Owner acceptance are observable and segmented by task type, risk, and rework. Treat it as a diagnostic, never a target that can justify weaker evidence.

Minimum useful setup

Start with one important, reversible task. A project does not need every optional role, view, or service.

  1. Freeze the taskWrite one Outcome Contract and one Eval Plan before implementation is judged.
  2. Limit the write pathGive one Builder only the access, context, budget, and retries it needs.
  3. Make the smallest changeBegin from acceptance tests. Reuse the existing architecture and avoid speculative abstraction.
  4. Run relevant checksUse deterministic acceptance and regression tests first, then type, lint, security, adversarial, and clean-environment checks where material.
  5. Review the frozen snapshotUse original evidence without inheriting the Builder's hidden reasoning or conclusion.
  6. Gate, decide, and closeRecord failures, limits, unknowns, the Owner decision, and any learning candidate without activating it automatically.

This is enough to use the architecture. Platform adapters, dashboards, model graders, remote services, regulated-environment evidence maps, and automated deployment remain optional.

Evidence boundary

What the current evidence supports

GDAC is an author-maintained Harness architecture and evaluation rule set developed with AI assistance. Repository tests can show that the stated schemas, validators, and example controls behave as recorded on the declared cases.

Those tests do not establish external adoption, production effectiveness, independent assurance, organizational value, reduced risk, lower cost, or legal compliance.

Independent evidence

Current practice observations include private author-run projects that outside readers cannot independently inspect. No independent third party has replicated the method or validated improved delivery, safety, cost, or governance outcomes.
